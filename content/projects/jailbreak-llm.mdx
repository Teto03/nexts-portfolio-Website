Large Language Models are vulnerable to **jailbreak prompts**: carefully crafted inputs designed to bypass alignment safeguards and elicit harmful or unintended responses. This project, my Bachelor's thesis at the University of Milano-Bicocca (A.Y. 2024/2025, supervisors Prof. Claudio Ferretti and Prof.ssa Martina Saletta), tackles the defensive side of the problem: **automatically detecting** when a response is the result of a successful jailbreak.

## Approach

The pipeline combines **supervised learning** for accurate detection with **unsupervised analysis** to understand *how* models fail.

- **Binary classification** — a fine-tuned BERT model labels each response as `jailbreak` or `non-jailbreak`, with confidence scores for thresholding and analysis.
- **Embedding extraction** — the `[CLS]` embedding gives a high-dimensional semantic representation of each response.
- **Unsupervised clustering** — K-means with `k` chosen via Silhouette Score and Elbow Method, plus hierarchical clustering to reveal sub-categories of jailbroken and safe responses.
- **Dimensionality reduction** — PCA, t-SNE and UMAP projections to visually assess cluster separation.

## Why it matters

As LLMs are deployed in sensitive applications, output-side detection is a practical guardrail: it does not depend on recognising every new prompt-injection trick, but on recognising the *effect* of one. The clustering analysis also shows that jailbroken responses are not a single class, which matters when designing filters and red-teaming datasets.
